# How free community content/tool hubs achieved reach — evidence for a reach-first playbook (free AE/Blender/Houdini/Nuke/Resolve script portal)

Research date: 2026-10-20. Method: web search summaries (WebFetch failed with DNS errors on every target, so most page contents were seen only via search-engine extraction). GitHub star counts were pulled live from the GitHub API on 2026-10-20 and are the most reliable numbers in this file. Labels used below: **[primary]** = the company or project's own page or post; **[3rd-party est.]** = Similarweb/Semrush or an analytics vendor; **[blog/low]** = SEO, affiliate or aggregator content; **[anecdote]** = a single person's account.

---

## 1. Case studies: growth numbers and the mechanisms behind them

### Takeaway
The hubs with the widest reach share three traits. Downloads are frictionless or anonymous, often under CC0/MIT. The catalog is installable from inside the host app (ComfyUI-Manager, Blender Extensions, Obsidian, Raycast, BlenderKit, nuBridge). And the index is open, machine-readable and usually on GitHub, so third parties can mirror and resurface it. Monetary or points rewards (MakerWorld, Printables) grow uploads fast but bring spam and moderation costs. Pure "free + no login + in-app" models (Poly Haven, the Obsidian plugin list, ComfyUI-Manager) reached millions of users with very small teams.

### Inferences
- Mechanisms ranked by how consistently they appear in the high-reach cases:
  1. **In-app install surface.** ComfyUI-Manager (multi-million downloads per node), Obsidian (100M+), BlenderKit (4M downloads/month), Raycast, Blender Extensions, nuBridge. AE equivalents are a ScriptUI/CEP/UXP panel; Blender has a remote repository.
  2. **Zero friction and permissive license.** Poly Haven: CC0, no account, about 2.7M users in 2022 rising to about 11M users later on its own stats.
  3. **Open, GitHub-hosted index or PR-based submission.** obsidian-releases, raycast/extensions, Greasy Fork's open code.
  4. **Curation and featuring.** CodePen's homepage picks, OpenProcessing curations, Ars Electronica-style recognition for Shadertoy.
  5. **Events and jams.** itch.io.
  6. **Points or money.** Printables, MakerWorld. These boost uploads, but MakerWorld's lead looks tied to Bambu's hardware install base as much as to points (inference).
- Many top hubs rode a parent ecosystem's install base or brand: Prusa and Bambu printers, Adobe for Mixamo, CSS-Tricks for CodePen, the Blender Foundation. A standalone script portal lacks one, so in-app panels and distribution through existing creators matter more.
- Long-tail concentration is extreme: the top 100 of about 8k Obsidian plugins take about 68% of downloads. A seed catalog of 30–100 high-utility scripts is worth more for launch reach than a large catalog.
- Incumbents decay when they are closed, neglected or unreliable (userscripts.org, Thingiverse, old Nukepedia). There is an opening in AE/VFX script hubs if the existing ones are paywalled, slow, or require login (cross-check against the other researchers' platform notes).

### Gaps
- No official time series for Blender Extensions downloads, the total Comfy Registry node count, or Raycast Store size; WebFetch was blocked, so stats pages could not be read directly.
- Poly Haven's "1.4M downloads per 3 months" vs "11M users" vs "126 TB/month" are inconsistent; the definitions are unknown.
- No reliable growth series for Greasy Fork site-wide installs, OpenProcessing, or Shadertoy over time.
- No source confirmed any specific YouTube-creator effect on Poly Haven. The search for Blender Guru/Poly Haven ties found that Blender Guru's founder runs a competitor, Poliigon ([Blender Guru donut v5](https://www.blenderguru.com/tutorials/blender-donut-v5-tutorial)).

## 2. Launch tactics with evidence, and post-mortems of failed hubs

### Takeaway
Show HN and Reddit produce short spikes (thousands to tens of thousands of visitors over 24–48 h) that rarely convert by themselves. Product Hunt has weakened sharply: most launches get under 10 votes, and only about 10% are featured. Durable reach in the cited cases came from (a) an established creator or blog channel (CodePen/CSS-Tricks), (b) recurring community events (itch.io jams, OpenProcessing open calls), and (c) a seeded catalog plus in-app presence. Failed hubs died from corporate deprioritization (Google Poly), maintainer abandonment (userscripts.org), or neglect and closed-source alienation (Thingiverse).

### Cited Findings
- **Show HN, individual post-mortem:** about 6k page views and about 500 unique visitors from HN, spiking for about 1.5 days and then a few dozen visits a day for a week, with no direct conversions. A first attempt failed because the title "sounded too producty" **[anecdote]** — [Indie Hackers: Front page of HN postmortem](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/front-page-of-hn-the-full-postmortem-traffic-lessons-surprises-cbe9e0a7f6)
- **Show HN, position benchmarks (14 launches):** #1 held for 12+ hours brings 10k–25k visitors, #2–5 bring 15k–20k, #21 brings 2.5k–3.5k. 58–68% of HN users block Google Analytics, so client-side analytics undercount **[blog, small sample]** — [Awesome Directories: HN front page guide](https://awesome-directories.com/blog/hacker-news-front-page-guide/)
- Plausible Analytics: HN was its #1 source (about 60% of all traffic through 2021), and its best HN day brought 94 trial signups. ### Inferences
- For a niche creative-tools portal, HN, PH and Reddit are one-off spikes worth doing for backlinks and branded search, not core channels. - Seed before announcing. Raycast and Blender both launched public stores with 100–200 items already in place.
- Survivability is itself a reach factor. Hubs that died (Poly, userscripts.org) were single-sponsor or single-maintainer; those that persisted kept open-source code, mirrors, or community funding (Greasy Fork, Poly Haven Patreon, Nukepedia nuBridge subscriptions). Publishing the catalog as a GitHub mirror protects against bus-factor collapse.

- No data on "build in public", newsletter, or conference-talk effects specifically for asset or script hubs; the SIGGRAPH relaunch of Nukepedia has no reported traffic figures.
- No Reddit launch data found for comparable hubs.

## 3. Reach trade-offs: login walls, licensing, mirroring, badges, open index/API, programmatic SEO, localization

### Takeaway
Every high-reach free hub examined lets users download without an account (Poly Haven) or installs inside the host app. Forced account creation is a well-documented source of drop-off in e-commerce (about 19–26% of abandoners cite it). The most permissive licenses (CC0 at Poly Haven, MIT on the Raycast monorepo) coincide with the most third-party redistribution. A static JSON index is a proven pattern for Blender (`index.json`, any remote repository), Obsidian (a JSON in GitHub) and Nukepedia (nuBridge loads the full DB), and it lets launchers and AI tools surface the catalog. Programmatic per-tool pages and localization show large but vendor-reported traffic gains.

### Cited Findings
- **Login wall vs anonymous:** Poly Haven states no account is needed — [Poly Haven License](https://polyhaven.com/license). Mixamo requires an Adobe ID, which excludes Enterprise/Federated IDs and users with a China country code — [Adobe Mixamo FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/lv/creative-cloud/faq/mixamo-faq.html). nuBridge (in-app) is gated behind a $5/month proUser subscription and shows 31 downloads on its listing — [nuBridge](https://www.nukepedia.com/tools/miscellaneous/nubridge/); [Digital Production Apr 2025](https://digitalproduction.com/2025/04/25/nukepedia-2-0-from-geekfest-to-gigabytes/)
- Forced account creation: commonly reported as a reason for about 19% of cart abandoners (other secondary sources say 24–26%), against about 70% average cart abandonment (Baymard) **[secondary, e-commerce proxy, not for free downloads]** — [Baymard: reduce cart abandonment](https://baymard.com/blog/reduce-cart-abandonment); [Red Stag](https://redstagfulfillment.com/percentage-of-online-shoppers-abandon-their-cart/); [LaunchMyStore](https://launchmystore.io/blog/how-to-reduce-shopping-cart-abandonment-rate)
- BlenderKit's free tier carried most of the volume: 105 TB of 120 TB of monthly downloads went to Free-plan users — [BlenderKit 300k](https://www.blenderkit.com/articles/blenderkit-reaches-300000-users/)
- **Licensing:** Poly Haven uses CC0 for everything — [License](https://polyhaven.com/license). The Raycast extensions platform and sources are MIT and on GitHub — [Raycast docs](https://developers.raycast.com/basics/contribute-to-an-extension); [raycast/extensions](https://github.com/raycast/extensions). Blender's official platform accepts only GPL-compliant software, which matters for AE/Blender script licensing if you mirror to it — [About Blender Extensions](https://extensions.blender.org/about/)
- **Static index files / open API:** Blender's `extension server-generate` builds `index.json` (plus an optional `--html` listing) from a folder of .zip files. Users add it via Get Extensions → Repositories → Add Remote Repository. The one strict requirement is that download URLs end in `.zip`, with optional `repository=` and `blender_version_min` query params for drag-and-drop installs **[primary]** — [Blender Manual: Static Extensions Repository](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/dev/advanced/extensions/creating_repository/static_repository.html)
- Obsidian's community plugin index is a JSON file in a public GitHub repo (22.4k stars, 7.9k forks) — [obsidian-releases](https://github.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-releases). Comfy Registry and Poly Haven expose public APIs used by third parties (for example, agent and asset directories index Poly Haven via its API) — [Comfy Registry docs](https://docs.comfy.org/registry/overview); [agentgid: Poly Haven API](https://agentgid.com/assets/poly-haven/)
- nuBridge loads all of Nukepedia (about 2,000 tools) into Nuke in 5–10 s, so a whole catalog index can stay small enough for client-side search — [befores & afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2025/08/10/nukepedia-is-making-a-comeback/)
- **Mirroring:** userscripts.org's content survived only because it was mirrored (userscripts-mirror.org) — [Userscript (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Userscript)
- **Programmatic SEO:** Zapier's per-integration pages reportedly drive about 2.3–2.6M monthly organic visits; for example, "Gmail integrations" brings about 60k/month. Calendly's integration pages are individually small (hundreds of visits each) but add up **[agency estimates, promotional]** — [upgrowth: Zapier pSEO](https://upgrowth.in/how-zapier-programmatic-seo-strategy-delivers-2-6m-monthly-organic-traffic/); [upgrowth: Calendly pSEO](https://upgrowth.in/how-calendlys-programmatic-seo-drives-1-1m-monthly-organic-traffic/); [TripleDart](https://www.tripledart.com/blog/programmatic-seo-for-saas)
- **Localization:** a vendor reports a 30–70% increase in impressions in the first months after adding a language (no sample size given). Case studies report +30% (Spanish to English agency site) and +102% (five Indian languages) **[vendor-reported]** — [Translated](https://translated.com/resources/organic-traffic-new-language); [WP Mayor](https://wpmayor.com/increase-traffic-by-translating-your-website/). Ahrefs (14 markets): content by local authors earns links, and naive translation underperforms — [Ahrefs: Localization SEO](https://ahrefs.com/blog/localization-seo/)
- **Badges / "Available on X":** no quantitative evidence found (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Reach-first defaults: anonymous one-click download; optional account only for uploading, favorites and notifications; CC0 or MIT as the default license for self-written and AI-written scripts, with author-selectable alternatives; and a public `index.json` (plus a GitHub mirror repo) carrying name, host app, versions, tags, download URL and a hash.
- Ship "native" indexes per host app: a Blender remote-repository `index.json` (zero-cost, official mechanism); a free AE ScriptUI/CEP/UXP launcher panel for the AE equivalent of ComfyUI-Manager and nuBridge (make it free, unlike nuBridge, since gating the in-app channel caps reach); and a Nuke/Houdini Python panel later.
- One page per script, per host app × version, and per task ("auto-crop comps After Effects script") is the pSEO pattern. These long-tail queries are where small niche sites can rank.
- Bilingual RU/EN is cheap given AI translation, but local-author content, not machine translation alone, earns links (Ahrefs). Use hreflang plus separate RU community channels (see Q6).

### Gaps
- No measured data found on author-embeddable badges ("Available on X") driving referrals; Flathub, Chrome Web Store and Raycast badge effects are unquantified in sources found.
- No A/B data on login walls for free asset or script downloads specifically; the Baymard data is an e-commerce proxy.
- No direct data on whether CC0 vs CC-BY changes download or redistribution volumes.
- No evidence found on the traffic effect of KBar-style launchers surfacing a third-party catalog.

## 4. Gamification evidence on creator and code hubs

### Takeaway
Gamification measurably changes behavior, but often not in the intended direction. GitHub's removal of streaks (May 2016) caused abandonment of long streaks and less weekend work, which shows streaks drove low-value "keep-alive" activity. Stack Overflow badges steer behavior near thresholds, though later work finds the effect is weaker or user-type-dependent. Duolingo's own tests show modest retention lifts (+14% Day-7 from Streak Wager). In creator hubs, points tied to downloads or prints (Printables, MakerWorld) attracted spam, low-effort remixes and constant rule changes. Money-tied rewards (Steam paid mods) can trigger backlash.

### Cited Findings
- **GitHub streaks (natural experiment; ICSE 2021):** after the counters were removed in May 2016, many long streaks were abandoned, long streaks became significantly rarer, weekend activity fell relative to weekdays, single-contribution days dropped, and streak synchronization among social neighbors fell (peer effects). The authors conclude gamification "can push developers in unexpected and unwanted directions" **[peer-reviewed]** — [Moldon et al., arXiv 2006.02371](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.02371); [ICSE 2021 proceedings](https://www.computer.org/csdl/proceedings-article/icse/2021/029600a549/1sEXoczR0TC); [code/data](https://github.com/lukasmoldon/GHStreaksThesis)
- GitHub achievements were studied exploratorily ("A lot of talk and a badge", Information and Software Technology 2024); details were not retrieved — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950584924001666)
- **Stack Overflow badges:** a formal model of badge steering, validated on Stack Overflow; badges can raise participation and shift activity mix **[peer-reviewed, WWW 2013]** — [Anderson, Huttenlocher, Kleinberg, Leskovec](https://archives.iw3c2.org/www2013/proceedings/p95.pdf). Follow-ups: the steering effect depends on user type — [Yanovsky et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2008.06125); some apparent steering is "phantom" (users would have reached the badge anyway) — [Phantom Steering Effect](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2002.06160); for much of the population the effect does not hold — [Springer KAIS 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10115-021-01637-6)
- **Duolingo streaks (company A/B tests):** Streak Wager raised Day-1/7/14 retention, with Day-7 up 14%. A Weekend Amulet made learners 4% more likely to return a week later and 5% less likely to lose their streak **[primary, company-reported]** — [Duolingo blog: How streaks keep learners committed](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals/)
- **Printables:** points for uploads, makes and downloads, redeemable for filament and merch — [Fabbaloo](https://www.fabbaloo.com/news/prusas-new-rewards-and-badging-system-wither-thingiverse). Prusa had to add anti-gaming rules (own designs only, limits on tiny few-gram miniatures, no duplicate photos) — [Prusa Help](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/how-do-prusameters-on-printables-work_365065). A 2023 community count reportedly found about 80% of Printables uploads finished vs about 45% on Thingiverse **[blog/low, unverified]** — [3dsearch.app](https://3dsearch.app/blog/printables-vs-thingiverse)
- **MakerWorld spam and downsides:** Bambu assigned moderators to New Uploads, removed uploads without photos of real prints, and penalized repeat violators — [Bambu forum: Actions against spamming of low-quality models](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/actions-taken-against-spamming-of-low-quality-models-on-makerworld/59572). Users complained about retroactive enforcement of changing rules — [Bambu forum: Retroactive enforcement](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/retroactive-enforcement-of-new-community-guidelines-needs-to-stop/100158). Low-effort "remixes" farm points, and the original designers get nothing — [same spam thread](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/actions-taken-against-spamming-of-low-quality-models-on-makerworld/59572?page=16). Creator sentiment includes "The Gold Rush is over — point system is dead" — [Bambu forum](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/the-gold-rush-is-over-point-system-is-dead/74148?page=2). The earning formula has been hidden since June 2025 — [BambuHub](https://bambuhub.net/guides/makerworld-points-rewards)
- MakerWorld's scale despite the turmoil: over 2.6M models, 280k+ creators (blog citing Bambu), and it out-trafficked Thingiverse by 2025–2026 — [StackSheriff](https://stacksheriff.com/3d-printing/makerworld-guide/); [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/makerworld.com/vs/thingiverse.com/)
- **Steam Workshop paid mods:** withdrawn after four days and a petition of over 51k signatures; Valve admitted it "underestimated" the differences from its earlier revenue-share models — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/valve-removes-paid-skyrim-mods-from-steam-workshop-admits-they-were-wrong/amp/); [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/events/steam-workshop-paid-modding)

### Inferences
- Lightweight, quality-gated recognition likely helps reach with little spam risk: "Featured" picks, "Verified works in AE 2025/2026" badges, author profiles with total downloads, and public per-script stats (as on Greasy Fork, Comfy Registry and Blender Extensions). Withhold points for raw uploads, especially with AI-written scripts, where the marginal cost of an upload is near zero and the MakerWorld-style spam dynamic would be amplified.
- If points are used, tie them to downstream verified use (installs from unique users, "it worked" confirmations, accepted fixes), not uploads. Give a share of a remix's credit to the original author (requested by the MakerWorld community).
- Avoid daily streaks for contributors (GitHub evidence of low-value keep-alive activity). For consumers, a "script of the day" or newsletter habit loop is lower-risk.

### Gaps
- No studies found on Kaggle progression tiers' effect on participation.
- No controlled before/after data on Printables upload volume when Prusameters launched (late 2021); only rough milestone figures exist.
- No data on Steam Workshop free-mod engagement metrics after 2015.

## 5. AI-era distribution: MCP registries, skills marketplaces, llms.txt, AI-assistant citations

### Takeaway
MCP and skills directories are huge and growing fast (official MCP Registry about 43k active servers by October 2026; Glama about 97k). Listing in the official registry propagates automatically to downstream directories. But I found no published evidence that a listing drives meaningful traffic or installs. llms.txt has strong negative evidence: crawlers rarely fetch it and it is not correlated with citations. AI referrals are still only about 0.25% of web referrals. The concrete AI-era reach signal for this niche is DCC-control MCP servers going viral on GitHub: Blender MCP has about 31.8k stars since March 2025; the leading After Effects MCP has about 0.8k. That suggests a "script catalog exposed as MCP tools/skills" could ride the same wave.

### Cited Findings
- **Official MCP Registry:** preview launched September 2025, metadata only (no code) **[secondary]** — [TrueFoundry: Best MCP registries](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/best-mcp-registries); [Tallyfy listing guide](https://tallyfy.com/how-to-list-mcp-server-registry-smithery-glama-pulsemcp/)

Glama showed 97,619 and Smithery over 26,700 the same day **[3rd-party census via public API]** — [LocalCan: State of MCP Server Development, Oct 2026](https://www.localcan.com/blog/state-of-mcp-server-development). mcp.so had about 20–22k (April–mid 2026) — [ThinkNEO comparison](https://thinkneo.ai/blog/mcp-registries-compared-20260714)

- **AI referral share:** AI chatbots were about 0.25% of worldwide referrals in August 2026 (peak 0.43% in April–May 2026, per Statcounter) — [upGrowth AI traffic report](https://upgrowth.in/ai-traffic-share-report-2026/). ChatGPT's share of AI referrals fell from 79.74% (2025) to 75.96% (2026) per SE Ranking — [SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/chatgpt-referral-traffic-may-2026/). Across studies, AI referrals are about 0.2–1.08% of visits — [MissionGrowth](https://missiongrowth.io/blog/ai-referral-traffic-statistics)

The top After Effects MCP, Dakkshin/after-effects-mcp, has 768 stars (created 2025-04-12) — [repo](https://github.com/Dakkshin/after-effects-mcp). - awesome-mcp-servers reached 96,667 stars within about 23 months (created 2024-11-30), showing GitHub lists remain the discovery layer for AI tools — [punkpeye/awesome-mcp-servers](https://github.com/punkpeye/awesome-mcp-servers)

### Inferences
- Cheap, high-optionality moves: publish one MCP server, "vfx-scripts-mcp", that searches the catalog index and installs or runs a script in AE/Blender. Register it in the official MCP Registry, which auto-propagates to Glama, PulseMCP and others, and PR it into awesome-mcp-servers. Package top scripts as Agent Skills (SKILL.md) for skills.sh and SkillsMP. Expect GitHub-star and community reach, not measurable web referrals.
- The demand signal is lopsided: Blender MCP has about 40x the stars of the best AE MCP. AI-driven AE automation is under-served, so a free "AE script catalog for AI agents" could become the default for that niche (inference; not validated).
- Skip llms.txt as a growth lever (cost is trivial, so it can exist, but expect nothing). Machine-readable per-script pages with clear metadata (JSON-LD, a stable `index.json`) are more likely to help both search engines and agents.

### Gaps
- No published data on referral traffic or installs from MCP-directory or skills-marketplace listings for any catalog.
- No data on how often AI assistants cite script or asset hubs (for example, Poly Haven or aescripts) in answers.
- Skills-directory counts are unreliable (scraped, inconsistent across sources).

## 6. Russian-language specifics: Telegram-first growth, Yandex SEO, VK, RU-born scaling

### Takeaway
Telegram was Russia's #1 messenger by daily reach (about 74M/day, December 2025; overtook WhatsApp in January 2026), but Roskomnadzor began throttling it on 2026-02-10 and almost fully blocked it in April 2026. Reach without a VPN fell to about 5%, though Mediascope still counted over 94M users in Q1 2026, and the effect on channel views varies widely (-52% to +1.5%). A RU strategy that relies only on Telegram is now a risky single point of failure, so RU reach must be multi-homed: Yandex SEO (about 70–72% of RU search), plus VK and possibly MAX, plus Telegram for the VPN-using, tech-savvy core (which likely overlaps heavily with AE/VFX professionals).

### Cited Findings
- **Telegram reach (Mediascope):** early 2025 monthly reach 74% of Russians aged 12+ (73% in 2024), daily 55%, 47 min/day, 9% of internet time — [ppc.world](https://ppc.world/news/auditoriya-telegram-v-rossii-snova-vyrosla/); [Hi-Tech Mail.ru](https://hi-tech.mail.ru/news/126521-74-rossiyan-polzuyutsya-telegram/). ### Inferences
- For a RU/EN AE/VFX script portal in late 2026: make the website plus Yandex SEO the RU backbone, using RU-language per-script pages, Yandex Webmaster and Yandex's structured data. Keep Telegram as the community and alerts channel, since VFX professionals likely already use VPNs (consistent with the smaller view losses for tech-savvy audiences). Add a VK community and VK Video mirror for non-VPN reach, and treat MAX as experimental.
- Auto-post every new or updated script to Telegram, VK and possibly MAX from the same `index.json` feed (the open-source auto-post bots show this is commodity tooling). That keeps the cost of multi-homing near zero.
- RU audiences historically scaled through Habr articles. A Habr launch post (for example, "a free open catalog of AE/Blender scripts, with an MCP server") is the RU analog of Show HN (inference; no quantitative case study found).

### Gaps
- No quantitative case study found of a RU-born content or tool catalog scaling via Telegram (search returned only tooling guides).
- No data gathered on VK or VK Video audience size or creative-pro usage in 2026 (VK was not researched in depth; the VK Video Wikipedia page surfaced but was not read).
- The October 2026 Telegram status in Russia is unclear (sources conflict on full block vs heavy throttling); the latest firm data points are Q1 2026 (94M users) and April 2026 (about 5% reachable without a VPN).
- No source found on Habr-launch traffic numbers for comparable open catalogs.

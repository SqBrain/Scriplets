# Script/Tool Distribution Ecosystems in 3D, VFX and Compositing Software (Blender, Houdini, Nuke, C4D, Maya/3ds Max, Resolve/Fusion, TouchDesigner, Unreal/Fab, Unity, Substance) — state as of Oct 2026

> Method note for the report writer: direct page fetching (WebFetch/curl) was blocked by the session's egress policy for every host tried (extensions.blender.org, sidefx.com, nukepedia.com, superhivemarket.com, strayspark.studio), and the shared web-search budget ran out near the end. All findings below come from web-search result summaries of the cited pages, so exact wording was not checked against the live page. Dates are given where the source showed them. "Est." marks estimates.

## Q1. What are the official and community distribution channels for each software?

### Takeaway
Only three of the host apps have an official, in-app, curated channel for third-party code: Blender (extensions.blender.org, free and GPL-only, since Blender 4.2 LTS in 2024), Unreal (Fab, paid and free, since Oct 2024), and Unity (Asset Store). Autodesk's App Store, now the "Design and Make Marketplace", is official but marginal for M&E. SideFX's Orbolt is widely seen as abandonware. Maxon, Blackmagic and Derivative run no third-party code store at all. Everywhere else, distribution is spread across community indexes (Nukepedia, ScriptSpot, Reactor/WeSuckLess, OLIB, qLib), GitHub, Gumroad and Patreon. Several of the old hubs have died or nearly died (CGSociety/CGTalk in Jan 2024, Highend3D/CreativeCrash on and off since 2020, VFXpedia around 2014).

### Cited Findings

#### Blender
- **Official: Blender Extensions Platform** — https://extensions.blender.org. It calls itself "the online directory of free and Open Source extensions for Blender" and only offers GNU GPL-compliant software — [extensions.blender.org/about](https://extensions.blender.org/about)
- Proposed in Oct 2022 — [code.blender.org 2022/10](https://code.blender.org/2022/10/blender-extensions-platform/). Beta released May 2024, when the add-ons and themes that used to ship with Blender moved onto the online platform — [code.blender.org 2024/05](https://code.blender.org/2024/05/extensions-platform-beta-release/). Generally available in Blender 4.2 LTS (2024) with "over 200 Extensions" at launch — [Blender 4.2 LTS release notes](https://www.blender.org/download/releases/4-2/)
- Built into Blender itself: Preferences > Get Extensions, with support for remote (third-party) repositories from 4.2 on — [Superhive docs: connecting as remote repository](https://support.superhivemarket.com/article/335-connecting-superhive-as-a-remote-repository-in-blender)
- **Main commercial channel: Superhive (formerly Blender Market)** — https://superhivemarket.com. Founded as Blender Market in 2014 (by CG Cookie, now operated by Autotroph). It was renamed Superhive in 2025 to follow Blender's trademark guidelines: the Foundation asks third parties not to put "Blender" in their names, and the old name was often mistaken for an official store — [Superhive rename post](https://superhivemarket.com/posts/beyond-business-as-usual-a-new-name-for-blender-market); [Blender: Reinforcing the trademark guidelines](https://www.blender.org/news/reinforcing-the-blender-trademark-guidelines/); [Blender on X praising the rebrand](https://x.com/Blender/status/1920137942032347627); [Say hello to Autotroph](https://superhivemarket.com/posts/say-hello-to-autotroph). Old blendermarket.com URLs redirect permanently — [VK post (RU)](https://vk.ru/@h360ru-blender-market-teper-nazyvaetsya-superhive)
- Superhive still positions itself as a Blender-tools store. No evidence was found of expansion into other host apps — [Superhive: "Blender Market is now Superhive"](https://superhivemarket.com/posts/blender-market-is-now-superhive-the-best-place-to-buy-and-sell-tools-for-blender)
- Other channels: Gumroad, Patreon (e.g., Poly Haven's add-on was paid on Superhive and is to be released free once it reaches 5,000 patrons — [Poly Haven blog](https://blog.polyhaven.com/liberating-our-blender-add-on/)), GitHub (topic "blender-extensions" — [GitHub topics](https://github.com/topics/blender-extensions)), CGTrader (assets; the vector for the 2025 malware campaign, see Q3). A 2026 indie "BlenderHunt" marketplace also exists — [chatgate.ai listing](https://chatgate.ai/post/blenderhunt) (low-authority source).

#### SideFX Houdini
- **Official store: Orbolt** — https://www.orbolt.com (launched about 2012, "Introducing Orbolt Smart 3D Assets" — [od|forum](https://forums.odforce.net/topic/15943-introducing-orbolt%E2%84%A2-smart-3d-assets/)). Run by SideFX and still listed inside Houdini's asset library. Forum users call it "abandonware", say SideFX "doesn't really maintain any distribution tools", report logins from inside Houdini failing, and note that many HDAs are outdated or have been superseded by native nodes — [SideFX forum: "Orbolt – dead or alive?"](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/92064/); [SideFX forum: "Time to dump Orbolt?"](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/72800/). No official shutdown announcement was found.
- HDAs made with the free Apprentice license can be sold on Orbolt (2021) — [Paul Ambrosiussen on X](https://twitter.com/ambrosiussen_p/status/1365750105865814019). Orbolt can convert Indie HDAs into commercial ones for Unreal, priced per license level. Gumroad sales, by contrast, reach only Indie users — [SideFX forum: Selling HDA on UE4 Marketplace](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/78044/)
- **Official free toolset: SideFX Labs** — "hundreds of HDAs, Python modules, game engine plugins, UI extensions, custom VEX functions". Developed outside the regular Houdini release cycle and open source on GitHub — [SideFX Labs product page](https://www.sidefx.com/products/sidefx-labs/); [Labs docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/labs/index.html); [Labs installation](https://www.sidefx.com/tutorials/sidefx-labs-installation/)
- **Community: qLib** — open-source HDA library under the New BSD license, built by VFX professionals at several studios. Main repo updated 6 Mar 2026 — [github.com/qLab/qLib](https://github.com/qLab/qLib); [qLab org](https://github.com/qLab); [qLib site](https://qlab.github.io/qLib/)
- **Community forum: od|forum (odforce)** — https://forums.odforce.net (qLib v0.1 announcement and install threads — [od|forum qLib v0.1](https://forums.odforce.net/topic/16518-qlib-v01/); [installing qLib package](https://forums.odforce.net/topic/44520-installing-qlib-package-method/))
- Paid HDAs are mostly sold on Gumroad (e.g., [AL-TOOLS Ultimate Terrain Workflow](https://adrienlambert.gumroad.com/l/NZtu)). Gumroad sellers like being able to ship a zip with hip files, HDAs and PDFs — [SideFX forum](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/72800/)

### Inferences
- The landscape splits three ways. (a) Host vendors with an in-app store: Blender (free only), Epic (Fab), Unity. (b) Host vendors with a nominal or neglected store: Autodesk, SideFX/Orbolt. (c) Host vendors with no store, where the community fills the gap: Foundry (Nukepedia), Blackmagic (Reactor/WSL), Derivative (OLIB/Patreon), Maxon (Gumroad/Boghma/Greyscalegorilla).
- In every ecosystem without a working official store, the de-facto channel is a one-person or small-volunteer community project (Nukepedia/Rueter, ScriptSpot, Reactor/WSL, OLIB/Hilker, qLib). These are single points of failure, as CGSociety, VFXpedia and Highend3D show.
- Paid code tools outside Blender and Unreal flow mostly through general-purpose creator platforms (Gumroad, Patreon) or the plugin-vendor storefronts (aescripts, Toolfarm). That leaves room for a cross-host "script store" with host-aware install.

## Q2. How big are these channels and what are their commission/revenue splits and pricing norms?

### Takeaway
Fab is by far the largest (420k+ listings, 20k+ publishers, $24M+ paid to creators in 2025, 88/12 split). Superhive is the largest dedicated "tools" market (about 7,500 creators, 60k+ products, nearly $50M paid out since 2014, creators keep 70–90%). Blender Extensions has about 900+ free add-ons and themes. Nukepedia has about 2,000 free tools, funded by a $5/month subscription. ScriptSpot has about 5,600 3ds Max scripts. Splits range from 0% commission (Autodesk historically, Boghma, Reactor) through 10% + $0.50 (Gumroad direct) and 12% (Fab) to 30% (Unity, Gumroad Discover, Superhive Free tier).

## Q3. How do channels handle moderation, code review, malware risk, versioning/compatibility, ratings, licensing and in-app installation/updates?

### Takeaway
Blender's platform has the most rigorous code policy: GPL-only, human review queue, no obfuscated code or remote code loading, declared permissions in a TOML manifest, and in-app install and updates that paid stores like Superhive can plug into as remote repositories. Fab does a technical build review for UE plugins but was still hit by a Maya worm in 2026. Community indexes (Nukepedia, ScriptSpot, Reactor) do little to no code vetting. Malware that rides on scene files (.blend Python, Maya scriptNodes) is a real, recurring risk. Install conventions differ per host: Blender manifest, Houdini packages JSON, Nuke init.py/menu.py with pluginAddPath, Reactor Atoms, C4D unique plugin IDs, OFX folders, TouchDesigner .tox drag-and-drop.

### Cited Findings

#### Blender (extensions.blender.org)
- 2022 platform proposal: uploads must be compatible with GPL v2.0 or v3.0; no obfuscated code; no remote code loading; user authorization required before any data is sent — [code.blender.org 2022](https://code.blender.org/2022/10/blender-extensions-platform/)
- In 2024 moderators made add-ons that use the bpy API GPL-3 only. Existing versions were grandfathered, but new updates must comply — approval-queue threads, e.g. [Discombobulator](https://extensions.blender.org/approval-queue/discombobulator/?version=0.1.0), [GScatter](https://extensions.blender.org/approval-queue/gscatter/), [Send2UE](https://extensions.blender.org/approval-queue/send2ue/), [Carver](https://extensions.blender.org/approval-queue/carver/), [World Apply](https://extensions.blender.org/approval-queue/world-apply/)
- Review practice: an extension can be put in "Awaiting Changes" for a missing description or manifest permissions. Reviewers also question bundled assets (assets are expected to be CC0). An add-on that needed a premium non-blender.org account was held back, because the platform "usually do[es] not accept extensions that require login and account in non-blender.org environments". Add-ons that previously shipped with Blender 4.1 were auto-approved without review — [approval queue: CloudRF](https://extensions.blender.org/approval-queue/cloudrf-3d/); [approval queue: Fast Language Switch](https://extensions.blender.org/approval-queue/fls-blcn/); [approval queue: my_Process](https://extensions.blender.org/approval-queue/my-process/)
- Manifest `blender_manifest.toml` (schema_version, id, version, name, tagline, maintainer, type add-on|theme). Optional permissions are declared for files, network, camera and microphone. The extension is packaged as a zip with Blender's CLI, then "held for review, and published once the moderation team approves it" — [Blender manual (dev): How to Create Extensions](https://docs.blender.org/manual/ja/dev/extensions/getting_started.html); [Blender manual 4.3](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.3/advanced/extensions/getting_started.html); [Creating Extensions](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/dev/advanced/extensions/)
- Per-version listings — [example versions page](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/preset-startup-manager/versions/)
- **Paid add-ons in-app (Superhive):** since Blender 4.2, dragging a purchase into Blender, with a one-time API token, adds Superhive as a remote repository. Purchases then appear under Preferences > Get Extensions, and updating is "as simple as clicking Update". Products without a valid manifest show as "Legacy Extension" and must be installed manually. Superhive auto-detects extension status from the bundled `blender_manifest.toml` — [Superhive announcement](https://superhivemarket.com/posts/blender-extension-support-launched-on-superhive); [Superhive docs: Extensions Support](https://support.superhivemarket.com/article/303-extensions-support); [Superhive docs: remote repository](https://support.superhivemarket.com/article/335-connecting-superhive-as-a-remote-repository-in-blender). Creators get a publishing add-on — [CGCookie/superhive-blender-addon](https://github.com/CGCookie/superhive-blender-addon)
- **Superhive Support Periods (live 22 Jun 2026):** opt-in per product. For 12 months after purchase, creators must answer support requests within 3 business days, give buyers access to new versions, and make a good-faith effort to stay compatible with the current Blender LTS. Non-participating products are sold "as is". Renewals are discounted — [Overview](https://support.superhivemarket.com/article/340-support-periods-overview); [Creator FAQ](https://support.superhivemarket.com/article/331-support-period-faqs-creators); [Customer FAQ](https://support.superhivemarket.com/article/330-support-period-faqs-customers); [Superhive launch post](https://superhivemarket.com/posts/support-periods-purchases-on-superhive-now-served-with-a-side-of-confidence); [3D Redbox Studio](https://www.3dredboxstudio.com/blog/superhive-support-period-from-the-sellers-side)
- Superhive's creator policy — [Creator Policy](https://support.superhivemarket.com/article/277-creator-seller-policy)
- **Malware:** StealC V2 infostealer campaign (reported Nov 2025). Malicious Python was embedded in .blend files on 3D-model marketplaces such as CGTrader and ran on open when "Auto Run Python Scripts" was enabled. It had been active for at least 6 months, was tied to Russian-speaking operators, and the sample had zero VirusTotal detections at analysis time. Advice given: treat .blend files like executables — [Morphisec](https://www.morphisec.com/blog/morphisec-thwarts-russian-linked-stealc-v2-campaign-targeting-blender-users-via-malicious-blend-files/); [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/malicious-blender-model-files-deliver-stealc-infostealing-malware/); [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/185052/cyber-crime/stealc-v2-malware-spread-through-weaponized-blender-files.html); [HivePro](https://hivepro.com/threat-advisory/stealc-v2-spreads-via-malicious-blender-files/)
- Bitdefender falsely flagged Superhive as a threat (forum thread) — [Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/superhive-formerly-blendermarket-detected-as-a-theat-by-bitdefender-antivirus/1590320)

#### Unreal / Fab
- Code plugins go through Epic's Technical Review Checklist. A third-party validator listed on Fab describes the checks: descriptor metadata (FriendlyName, Category, SupportURL, EngineVersion, FabURL), packaging (no build/intermediate folders, Installed=false, 128px icon, FilterPlugin.ini), source hygiene (copyright headers), and a clean RunUAT BuildPlugin compile, "the same clean build Fab runs on submission" — [RTC Validator listing on Fab](https://www.fab.com/listings/c51b8825-6110-49b5-b029-64260bc8f13e) (secondary)
- Ratings: at launch, UE Marketplace star ratings migrated but text reviews and Q&A were not supported. By the end of 2025, buyers could leave both star ratings and written reviews — [Fab 2025 Year in Review](https://www.unrealengine.com/news/fab-2025-year-in-review); [GameFromScratch](https://gamefromscratch.com/epic-games-make-massive-fab-announcements/); [Fab dev update, 3 Jun 2025](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/fab-development-update-june-3-2025-faq-individual-sales-metahuman-and-more/2531976)
- Licensing: Standard vs CC-BY. The "NoAI" tag (no gen-AI training) is allowed only under the Standard license — [Fab docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/fab/licenses-and-pricing-in-fab?lang=en-US). A mandatory "Created with AI" self-declaration flag is reported by a secondary source (unverified) — [Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/ai-game-assets-what-studios-own-2026/). The EU DSA trader-status declaration blocked some plugin downloads in the EU (e.g., Adobe Substance 3D for UE) — [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-64/substance-3d-for-unreal-engine-unavailable-631558)
- **Malware incident:** AV-Comparatives found a self-replicating Maya Python worm, "MEL/Vacphage.A", in Maya scene files uploaded unknowingly by 39 Fab sellers. #### Autodesk Maya / 3ds Max
- Marketplace product guidelines are per host. A 3ds Max plug-in must be "relevant to (and usable with) 3ds Max 2027". A Maya product with a licensing system must "allow your product to run as soon as it is installed" — [3ds Max publishing guidelines](https://aps.autodesk.com/app-store/publisher-center/3ds-max); [Maya guidelines](https://aps.autodesk.com/ja/app-store/publisher-center/maya); [Product Guidelines](https://apps.autodesk.com/Publisher/ProductGuidelines)
- **Maya Security Tools** (free Autodesk plug-in): detects and removes malicious scripts in .ma/.mb files, blocks them from being saved, and works in batch/command-line modes. #### Houdini
- Packages: JSON files in `HOUDINI_PATH/packages` that modify the environment (studio, project, user or per tool). They must be valid JSON with the .json extension. SideFX Labs itself loads this way (sidefx_packages/SideFXLabs*.json) — [SideFX docs: Houdini packages](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/ref/plugins.html). Studios share otls folders through packages JSON — [The Technical Artist](https://thetechnicalartist.com/houdini/hdas.html); community templates — [ariffjeff/houdini-packages](https://github.com/ariffjeff/houdini-packages); [James Robinson package template](https://www.jamesrobinsonvfx.com/projects/houdini-package-template/)
- Orbolt treats different HDA versions as separate assets, refunds are hard, and its in-Houdini tools reportedly break — [SideFX forum](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/72800/)

#### Nuke
- Nuke runs `init.py` and then `menu.py` in each plug-in path directory. UI code belongs only in menu.py. Paths are added with `nuke.pluginAddPath` (prepend) or `pluginAppendPath`, or with the NUKE_PATH env var. Users usually work in `~/.nuke`, and Foundry advises against editing the install's own init.py/menu.py. There is no formal gizmo convention beyond a `gizmos` subfolder — [Foundry: Installing Plug-ins](https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/developers/90/pythondevguide/installing_plugins.html); [Foundry: Start-up Scripts](https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/developers/80/pythondevguide/startup.html); [Foundry: Loading Gizmos, NDK Plug-ins, Python and Tcl](https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/content/comp_environment/configuring_nuke/loading_gizmos_plugins_scripts.html); [Foundry support article](https://support.foundry.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003811839)
- Nukepedia lists entries with per-entry download counts and version compatibility, e.g., the nuBridge listing requires Nuke 13.0+ — [Nukepedia: Other gizmos](https://www.nukepedia.com/tools/gizmos/other/); [nuBridge](https://www.nukepedia.com/tools/miscellaneous/nubridge/)
- Cattery models load from a Cattery folder with an automatic menu — [Nukepedia: Cattery Nuke13 support](https://www.nukepedia.com/tools/python/misc/cattery_nuke13_support/)

#### Fusion / Resolve
- Reactor Atoms are declarative package files in a Git repo that Reactor syncs, so users don't copy files by hand — [Creating Atom Packages](https://gitlab.com/WeSuckLess/Reactor/-/blob/master/Docs/Creating-Atom-Packages.md); [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2018/05/we-suck-less-ships-reactor-2-0/)
- OFX plugins install to `/Library/OFX/Plugins` (macOS) or `C:\Program Files\Common Files\OFX\Plugins` (Windows). DCTLs go into Resolve's LUT directory — [Gyroflow docs](https://docs.gyroflow.xyz/app/video-editor-plugins/davinci-resolve-openfx); [Reuleaux install](https://github.com/hotgluebanjo/reuleaux/blob/master/doc/installation.md)

#### Cinema 4D
- Plugins must register a globally unique ID from the Plugin Café generator. If an ID is reused, only one of the plugins claiming it loads at startup — [c4d.plugins docs](https://developers.maxon.net/docs/py/2026_1_0/modules/c4d.plugins/); [Plugin Identifiers](https://developers.maxon.net/forum/pid/)

#### TouchDesigner
- Install means dragging the .tox into the network root. Pulling components from the web inside TD is possible through the OLIB Browser — [FunctionStore_tools](https://github.com/function-store/FunctionStore_tools); [alltd.org OLIB Browser](https://alltd.org/touchdesigner-component-olib-browser/); [YouTube: Tox Files](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DEc_BOQsLGM)

#### Unity
- Asset Store submissions must disclose AI use (including AI-generated code), list the tools in an "AI description" field, and follow a two-strike policy for undisclosed AI content. Submissions are rejected for anatomical errors, copyright resemblance, plagiarism, or "not offering significant value" — [Unity Asset Store Content Transparency](https://unity.com/legal/asset-store-content-transparency); [Legacy content policy](https://unity.com/legal/asset-store-content-transparency-legacy)

### Inferences
- Blender's stack is the most mature model for a portal to copy or interoperate with: a manifest with declared permissions, a human review queue, a licence rule, in-app install, and a remote-repository protocol that third-party (paid) stores can plug into.
- Both major 2025–26 malware cases (StealC in .blend files on CGTrader; the Maya worm on Fab) travelled inside scene files, not inside declared add-ons, and evaded signature-based detection. A script portal should therefore (a) never auto-execute embedded code, (b) scan for known scene-script patterns (Maya scriptNode/scriptJob, .blend text blocks with auto-run), and (c) be ready to notify buyers and pull listings.
- Each host has its own install format, so a cross-software portal needs a per-host "install adapter" layer: generate a Blender manifest/remote repo, a Houdini package JSON, Nuke menu.py snippets/NUKE_PATH, a Reactor Atom, C4D plugin folder, OFX/DCTL paths, or a .tox drop. Reactor and nuBridge both show that users will adopt an in-app manager when it exists.

### Gaps
- Nukepedia's and ScriptSpot's moderation/code-review practices were not found (likely light manual approval, unconfirmed).
- Whether Fab scans code plugins for malware, or only build-checks them, was not found in official docs.
- Detailed Blender manifest version-compatibility keys (e.g., blender_version_min) were not confirmed from the docs in search results.
- No information on Orbolt's review process.

## Q4. Which channels are cross-software aggregators, and how do they structure categories by host application?

### Takeaway
True cross-host aggregators of code tools are few. Fab became multi-DCC in 2025 (Unreal plus Unity, and reportedly Blender/Maya/Houdini/Godot tools). Getly (2026) is a newcomer pitching multi-host plugin sales.

## Q5. What works well, what are the known pain points, and what lessons apply to a new cross-software portal?

### Takeaway
What works: in-app install and updates (Blender Extensions plus Superhive remote repos, Reactor, nuBridge, OLIB Browser), clear licence rules (Blender GPL, Fab Personal/Professional, NoAI tag), free content funded by convenience subscriptions (Nukepedia), and visible ecosystem giving (Superhive → Blender Fund). Pain points: hub fragility and knowledge loss (CGSociety 2024, VFXpedia about 2014, Highend3D 2020, ScriptSpot's 2026 migration freeze, Nukepedia's Joomla end-of-life), store neglect (Orbolt), migration damage (Fab: sales drops, search invisibility, spam, per-seat confusion, payout delays), unsustainable "pay once, update forever" economics (Superhive Support Periods controversy), and malware in scene files.

### Cited Findings
#### Works well
- In-app one-click install and update of paid Blender add-ons through Superhive's remote repository — [Superhive announcement](https://superhivemarket.com/posts/blender-extension-support-launched-on-superhive)
- Reactor removes manual file copying through Git-synced Atoms, and runs in Fusion, Resolve and as a standalone app — [GitLab Reactor](https://gitlab.com/WeSuckLess/Reactor); [Reactor-Standalone](https://github.com/Kartaverse/Reactor-Standalone)
- nuBridge, an in-app "app store" for 2,000 free Nukepedia tools, paid for through a $5/month subscription; universities and facilities have asked for site licences — [Digital Production 2026](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/07/13/nukepedia-rebuilt-nubridge-reloaded/); [Nukepedia Pro](https://www.nukepedia.com/subscription/pro)
- Derivative's docs officially endorse a community library (OLIB), lending it legitimacy at no cost — [TouchDesigner docs](https://docs.derivative.ca/Main_Page)
- Fab added star ratings plus written reviews and rebuilt search on historical data in 2025 — [Fab 2025 YIR](https://www.unrealengine.com/news/fab-2025-year-in-review)
- ScriptSpot's migration kept every old URL working and pruned nothing — [ScriptSpot](https://scriptspot.com/)

#### Pain points
- **Fab migration (2024–25):** long-time sellers report big sales drops, products missing from search and the market page, "Recent Releases" flooded by sellers who "spam dozens of small material/prop assets every day", no way to publish a plugin that depends on the seller's own other plugin, and per-seat code-plugin licensing with no multi-seat purchase option — [Epic forum: Fab feedback](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/fab-feedback-on-what-should-be-improved/2468636). Some sellers saw months of missing payouts around the launch — [itch.io devlog](https://petyu.itch.io/uedatamosh/devlog/804345/issues-with-payouts-and-new-update-delay). Other reports: "many sellers report having no sales since Fab launched", and Fab "a pretty hostile place for … sellers at launch" — [Epic forum: Fab sales report](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/fab-sales-report/2089110); [techarthub](https://techarthub.com/my-unreal-marketplace-journey/). At launch, free assets were re-licensed as Standard, which raised commercial-use confusion — [Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/all-free-assets-including-ue-ones-are-now-standard-license-on-fab-meaning-no-commercial-use/2080626)
- **Orbolt neglect:** "horrible and frustrating user experience", login failures, deprecated HDAs, a dated look; sellers find Gumroad easier even for HDAs — [SideFX forum 92064](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/92064/); [SideFX forum 72800](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/72800/)
- **Hub fragility and knowledge loss:** CGSociety's closure (8 Jan 2024) wiped 18,000+ MAXScript threads, and the Wayback Machine reportedly captured little — [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/234700/the-largest-archive-of-maxscript-knowledge-online-is-about-to-be-lost-forever). Highend3D went down after an acquisition dispute — [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/217267/highend3d-formerly-creative-crash-is-gone). VFXpedia's disappearance broke Fusion script links — [Blackmagic forum](https://forum.blackmagicdesign.com/viewtopic.php?p=225749). Nukepedia's Joomla platform became unsustainable and the relaunch slipped about a year (SIGGRAPH 2025 target → live by mid-2026) — [Digital Production Apr 2025](https://digitalproduction.com/2025/04/25/nukepedia-2-0-from-geekfest-to-gigabytes/); [Digital Production Jul 2026](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/07/13/nukepedia-rebuilt-nubridge-reloaded/). Rueter frames the work as a "quest to stop Nuke knowledge from disappearing" — [Digital Production Jul 2025](https://digitalproduction.com/2025/07/23/frank-rueters-quest-to-stop-nuke-knowledge-from-disappearing-nubridge/)
- **Creator sustainability (Blender):** Superhive's CEO-level essay (Jonathan Williamson, 21 Apr 2026, first given as a BCON Austin talk) calls the Blender creator ecosystem "in crisis" and introduced 12-month Support Periods with paid renewals. Community press called the change controversial — [Superhive essay](https://superhivemarket.com/posts/we-built-this-together-now-we-need-to-fix-it); [Autotroph mirror](https://autotroph.com/we-built-this-together-now-we-need-to-fix-it); [Support Periods rationale](https://superhivemarket.com/posts/support-periods-a-sustainable-path-forward-for-blender-creators); [BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2026/04/01/incoming-policy-changes-on-superhive/); [Foro3D](https://foro3d.com/en/2026/abril/superhive-limita-el-soporte-a-12-meses-y-genera-polemica-en-la-comunid.html)
- **Brand confusion:** "Blender Market" was mistaken for an official Blender store, which forced a rebrand in 2025 — [Blender trademark post](https://www.blender.org/news/reinforcing-the-blender-trademark-guidelines/)
- **Blender platform limits:** GPL-only and no external-account requirement, so commercial and SaaS-connected add-ons are pushed to third-party repos — [approval queue: CloudRF](https://extensions.blender.org/approval-queue/cloudrf-3d/). Early reception was mixed ("stud or dud?") — [CGDive](https://cgdive.com/new-blender-extensions-platform/); [devtalk feedback thread](https://devtalk.blender.org/t/extensions-platform/34663)
- **Stale compatibility:** Highend3D packs list support only up to Maya 2017, and some are flagged as not working in 2018 — [Highend3D listing](https://www.highend3d.com/maya/script/free-130-scripts-for-modeling-uv-video-tutorials-for-each-for-maya)
- **Malware in scene files:** StealC .blend campaign (Nov 2025) and Maya worm on Fab (2026); see Q3 — [Morphisec](https://www.morphisec.com/blog/morphisec-thwarts-russian-linked-stealc-v2-campaign-targeting-blender-users-via-malicious-blend-files/); [AV-Comparatives](https://av-comparatives.org/maya-malware-fab-com/)

### Inferences
1. **Be host-native at install time.** Every success story (Blender remote repos, Reactor, nuBridge, OLIB Browser, Fab UE plugin) installs and updates inside the host. A cross-software portal should ship per-host connectors (Blender remote-repo JSON API, a Houdini package generator, a Nuke menu.py/NUKE_PATH bootstrap, Reactor-style manifests for Fusion, a C4D plugin folder, a TD .tox browser) rather than plain zip downloads.
2. **Use a mandatory manifest with host plus version-range compatibility and declared permissions** (modelled on blender_manifest.toml). This addresses the stale-compatibility problem (Highend3D, Orbolt) and gives users security transparency.
3. **Plan for durability and portability.** Guarantee permanent URLs and an export/archive (ScriptSpot's "old links still work"), and avoid single-maintainer dependence. CGSociety's loss shows a portal can win trust by mirroring or archiving at-risk knowledge.
4. **Scan uploads, including sample scenes** (Maya scriptNodes/scriptJobs, .blend auto-run text blocks, obfuscated or remote-loading code, per Blender's 2022 rules), and keep a buyer-notification and takedown process (Epic took up to 90 days).
5. **Set clear licence and update terms.** Offer Blender-style licence options, Fab-style buyer tiers, a NoAI option, and explicit update/support windows (Superhive Support Periods) so buyers know what they get. Also allow per-seat/studio licences, which Fab fails to sell for code plugins.
6. **Curate discovery against asset spam.** Fab's "Recent Releases" flooding suggests rate-limits, per-seller caps, or separate feeds for code tools versus bulk assets.
7. **Revenue-share benchmarks:** at least 80% for creators to compete with Fab (88%) and Superhive (70–90%). Optional "donate to host foundation" mechanics (Superhive → Blender Fund) build goodwill with open-source host communities.
8. **Use a neutral brand.** Don't name the portal after a host app (Blender Market → Superhive lesson, trademark guidelines).
